The short version

Written for someone with five minutes.

What we found

Stays last 15.5 days on average, and one in five patients stays 25 days or longer. That is the biggest lever in the file. Cutting just one day per stay would free about bed-days. Billing averages $25,539 per stay but has no link to stay length or condition, which is a billing integrity flag. No group differs from another in any statistically meaningful way.

What we can't tell you

The file has no readmissions, outcomes, patient IDs or costs. So readmission, mortality and cost-per-case cannot be measured. The perfectly even spread across every field suggests the data may be synthetic or heavily anonymised. Treat it as a practice dataset unless you can confirm it comes from live systems.

Statistical tests: is any difference real?

p-values. A value below 0.05 would signal a real difference. None do.

Correlations are all near zero: age vs. stay 0.008, age vs. bill −0.004, stay vs. bill −0.006. Longer stays should cost more. Here they don't, so billing is not tied to the care delivered.

Phase 4 · Five opportunities

Ranked by impact. Confidence reflects how much the data can really support the claim.

1 · Shorten long stays Medium confidence

  • Shows: 15.5-day average; 20.1% of stays (≈11,150) last 25–30 days.
  • Affects: every condition and insurer equally, so it is a system-wide pattern.
  • Impact: one day less per stay frees about 55,500 bed-days over 5 years, roughly 11,100 a year or 30 beds continuously occupied.
  • Why only medium: no diagnosis severity, so we can't say which stays were avoidable.

2 · Fix billing integrity High confidence

  • Shows: 534 duplicate rows (≈$13.4M), 108 negative bills (−$53.9k), 426 bills under $1,000.
  • Impact: up to $13.4M in overstated revenue, or duplicate charges, to verify.
  • Why high: these are countable, not statistical.

3 · Link price to care Medium confidence

  • Shows: billing is uncorrelated with stay length (−0.006) and condition (p = 0.056, borderline).
  • Impact: unknown until a charge master or cost data is added. Possible under- or over-billing.

4 · Repair identifiers for readmission tracking High confidence

  • Shows: 49,992 distinct names for 55,500 rows, mangled capitalisation, no patient ID.
  • Impact: readmissions, the standard quality KPI, cannot be measured at all today.

5 · Follow up on abnormal results Low confidence

  • Shows: 18,627 abnormal and 18,356 inconclusive results, 66.6% together.
  • Impact: inconclusive tests often mean repeat testing and extra days. We can't prove it here.

Phase 5 · Why might this be happening?

Hypotheses to test, not conclusions. The data cannot prove causes.

Flat patterns everywhere

  • Synthetic or randomised data. Test: compare to the live source system, check generation notes.
  • Over-anonymisation. Test: ask data owners what was shuffled.
  • Genuine standardisation. Unlikely given uniform billing; test with clinical review.

Long stays

  • Discharge delays. Test: add discharge-ready timestamps.
  • Severity not captured. Test: add DRG or ICD-10 codes.
  • Data entry errors in dates. Test: audit a sample of 100 charts.

Billing oddities

  • Duplicate load from the system. Test: check ETL logs.
  • Credits entered as negatives. Test: finance review of 108 rows.

What the data proves vs. suggests

Proves: counts, averages, duplicates, no significant group differences. Suggests: a systemic LOS issue and weak billing linkage. Cannot say: anything about quality or outcomes.

Limitations & what to ask next

Limitations

  • No patient ID, so one row may not equal one person.
  • No cost data: billing is not cost.
  • Benchmarks (5–6 day acute stay) are general rules of thumb, not tied to your case mix.
  • Observational data: nothing here shows cause and effect.
  • Under-18 group is small (116).

Five questions to sharpen this

  1. Is this real, de-identified or synthetic data?
  2. Can you provide a patient ID and readmission flag?
  3. What are diagnosis codes and DRGs for each stay?
  4. What does Billing Amount represent: charge, payment or cost?
  5. Which units or sites should we compare, and how are they coded?
